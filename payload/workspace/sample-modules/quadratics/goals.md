# Goals — Quadratics Module

## Mastery target (extended encoding, §4)

> Given the geometric definition of a parabola (every point equidistant from a focus and a directrix), derive the standard form `y = ax² + bx + c` and the vertex form `y = a(x - h)² + k`, and explain why both describe the same curve.

This is the schema-building peak. The learner has already done this once — the module should make that derivation reproducible from a cold start, and extend it.

## Minimum bar to advance from the module (§9)

The learner can:

1. Factor any quadratic with integer roots without hesitation
2. Solve `ax² + bx + c = 0` by factoring, completing the square, and the quadratic formula
3. Convert between standard form and vertex form in both directions
4. Sketch the parabola from either form, identifying vertex, axis of symmetry, and roots
5. Explain in plain language **why** completing the square works

## Ideal bar (real mastery, §9)

The learner can additionally:

1. Derive the quadratic formula from completing the square
2. Derive vertex form from the geometric definition of a parabola
3. Derive standard form from vertex form by expansion, and explain the algebraic identity that connects `h, k` to `a, b, c`
4. Name at least two domains where quadratics appear (projectile motion, optimization, conic sections) and explain why

## Metacognition checks per node (§9)

Each node carries three checks:
- **Explanation** — can you explain the how and why?
- **Transfer** — can you use this in a context not in the source material?
- **Connection** — can you name two other nodes this connects to and articulate the relationship?

## Non-goals

- Memorizing the quadratic formula without being able to derive it — explicitly rejected per §2 (mastery ≠ memorization)
- Speed on contrived problems — fluency is the floor, not the goal
