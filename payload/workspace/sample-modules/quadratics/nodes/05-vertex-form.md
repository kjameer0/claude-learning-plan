# 05 — Vertex Form

## Prereqs
- `03 standard-form`
- Function transformations: `f(x - h)` shifts right by `h`, `f(x) + k` shifts up by `k`

## Enables
- `06 completing-the-square`
- `08 parabola-geometry`

## What this node is

`y = a(x - h)² + k`, where `(h, k)` is the vertex of the parabola. Vertex form **makes the vertex obvious** at the cost of hiding the y-intercept.

## Sources
- Algebra 1 textbook: "Vertex form of a quadratic"
- Desmos: plot `y = a(x - h)² + k`, slide each parameter

## Fluency exercises

1. State the vertex: `y = (x - 3)² + 2`, `y = -2(x + 1)² - 4`, `y = (x - 5)²`
2. Sketch `y = (x - 2)² + 1` by hand. Mark vertex, axis of symmetry, one point on each side.
3. Convert to standard form by expanding: `y = 2(x - 1)² + 3`

## Schema-building exercises

1. **Why `(x - h)` and not `(x + h)`?** Sketch `y = (x - 3)²` and `y = (x + 3)²`. Both are parabolas, both have minimum value 0. Where is the vertex of each? Explain why the sign flips between the formula and the graph.
2. **What does `a` do, geometrically?** Compare `y = (x - 2)²`, `y = 2(x - 2)²`, `y = 0.5(x - 2)²`, `y = -(x - 2)²`. Describe `a` as a vertical stretch and orientation flip.
3. **Vertex form ↔ standard form.** Expand `y = a(x - h)² + k` symbolically. Read off `b` and `c` in terms of `a, h, k`. What does this say about how the vertex relates to the coefficients of standard form?

## Metacog checks
- **Explanation**: Why is `(h, k)` the vertex? Explain using function transformations from `y = x²`.
- **Transfer**: Given a parabola with vertex `(-3, 4)` passing through `(0, 1)`, find its equation in vertex form.
- **Connection**: How does vertex form connect to `06 completing-the-square` (algorithm to get there) and `08 parabola-geometry` (where the vertex comes from a geometric definition)?
